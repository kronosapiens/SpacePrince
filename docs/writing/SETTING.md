# Cosmic fable and setting

Space Prince is a cosmic fable in a science-fantasy universe.
Technology and magic coexist, and their workings can remain mysterious.
Encounters give the player glimpses of varied worlds and inhabitants through recognizable concerns and pleasures.
Atmosphere carries most of the setting; technical explanations and detailed histories are rarely needed.

[VOICE.md](VOICE.md) guides prose, cadence, and reflection.
[SCENES.md](SCENES.md) governs scene construction, choices, and review.

## References

These works contribute different qualities to the writing.

### Siddhartha

When Govinda asks Siddhartha to share what he has learned, Siddhartha distinguishes knowledge that can be taught from wisdom that must be lived. [Hermann Hesse, *Siddhartha*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2500/pg2500-images.html)
Carry forward openness to experience and partial understanding.
Each encounter offers something the player can attend to and interpret for themselves.
The game's scenes stand alone in any order; they do not reproduce the novel's sequence of spiritual development.

### The Prophet

A mason asks about houses, and the book's reflections repeatedly return to work, food, family, and the things people make and use. [Kahlil Gibran, *The Prophet*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58585/pg58585-images.html)
Keep that closeness between reflection and ordinary life across unfamiliar settings.
The [passages in VOICE.md](VOICE.md) show how physical things can give a thought substance.
Gibran's influence leaves room for speakers with different manners and convictions.

### Hyperion

Seven pilgrims approach a shared destination with private hopes and unresolved histories. [Dan Simmons, *Hyperion*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/167468/hyperion-by-dan-simmons/)
Use the variety of travellers' perspectives to suggest a universe larger than any single encounter.
Different lives can reveal different facets of that universe while each scene supplies its own context and significance.

### Star Wars

Pilots spend their downtime in the [Mos Eisley cantina](https://www.starwars.com/databank/mos-eisley-cantina), while the [Force](https://www.starwars.com/databank/the-force) gives spiritual mystery a place among ships and machines.
Take the ease with which unfamiliar beings and extraordinary powers inhabit an understandable world.
Local customs, worn objects, and practical complaints can make a place feel familiar before the reader knows much about it.

## Establish a place through particulars

Choose a few details that the reader can picture or feel: food, architecture, weather, clothing, bodies, or ways of working.
Let inhabitants treat familiar wonders casually.
An object can be cherished, inherited, unreliable, or repaired without an account of how it works.
Use familiar language for its place in someone's life.

Vary the worlds, species, and material surroundings across the catalogue.
Domestic life, gardens, craft, and ritual can share the universe with vessels and machines.
Give characters individual wants and disagreements; a culture need not speak with one voice or embody one idea.
Wonder can accompany humor, embarrassment, satisfying work, or uncomplicated pleasure.

## Keep participation understandable

The setting may provide atmosphere or shape the choice directly.
A remarkable detail need not become the scene's central problem.
When a strange condition affects participation, make its practical consequences clear enough to act on.
The reader should understand what someone wants and what the Prince's response would change, even while the underlying mechanism remains mysterious.

Give the people here particular reasons to care.
A character's belief can be incomplete or disputed, and the Prince's interpretation remains the player's.
The word "fable" describes the intimate scale and room for reflection; an encounter need not deliver a moral.

## Make every encounter complete in itself

Write each scene to be meaningful wherever it appears in a run.
Supply its relationships, stakes, and possible participation within the encounter.
Its meaning should be available without earlier meetings, a prescribed character arc, or a later payoff.

Shared concerns such as hospitality, pride, obligation, and departure can recur in different circumstances.
Familiar textures and the prose voice give independent scenes a sense of belonging together.
The player may find connections between encounters, but each encounter should hold interest on its own.
