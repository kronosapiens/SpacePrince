# Mercury in the houses: research notes

Preliminary literary research for eleven placements outside House X, plus an independent Jupiter VI comparison, consulted 2026-10-04.
The [full matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md) contains the lead synthesis after review; proposals here preserve the independent research stage.
Source summaries describe astrological interpretations; every dynamic, expression, and scene below is an original game proposal.
The Prince's own Mercury occupies the house; rulership, joy, and house–sign equations supply no placement evidence here.
Signs can vary manner after the house situation and planetary agency are established.

## I — Self

**Sources:** Heese I: changeable presentation and curiosity; Reed I: speech makes an immediate impression.

**Original dynamic:** Arriving gives the Prince an opportunity to try a way of being through speech, gesture, and quick response.
**Expression A:** He enjoys discovering what he can make of an unfamiliar introduction.
**Scene A:** A host introduces every traveler through an improvised riddle and leaves the Prince's ending unfinished for him to invent.
**Expression B:** He resents an old acquaintance anticipating his every answer and wants to become less predictable.
**Scene B:** Someone greets him with a practiced imitation of his usual flourish; he can play along, answer differently, or let the greeting hang.

## II — Livelihood

**Sources:** Heese II: practical appraisal at one's own pace; Reed II: financial preoccupation and business acumen.

**Original dynamic:** Understanding what things can do becomes a means of obtaining what will sustain him.
**Expression A:** He takes pleasure in a bargain built from uses others have overlooked.
**Scene A:** A provisioner rejects a cracked basin, but the Prince notices its halves would fit the shop's two leaking grain chutes.
**Expression B:** He wants time to judge an offer without being hurried into someone else's valuation.
**Scene B:** A buyer keeps raising the price for his tool while refusing to explain its unusual maker's mark; he can sell or investigate what he holds.

## III — Communication

**Sources:** Heese III: news and many interests; Reed III: correspondence and gossip.
Firmicus III.7.6 includes discoveries achieved beyond received tradition; subsequent Jupiter/Mars conditions add separate claims and are not imported.

**Original dynamic:** Familiar routes and conversations can carry both delicious news and discoveries nobody has yet named.
**Expression A:** He wants to be present when a neighborhood story acquires its next improbable detail.
**Scene A:** Three neighbors offer different accounts of the same runaway goat, and the Prince can keep the enjoyable disagreement moving.
**Expression B:** He wants to test a pattern that familiar explanations have missed.
**Scene B:** The village's tapping pipes repeat an unfamiliar sequence whenever the mill stops; he recruits a nearby listener to answer from the other end.

## IV — Home

**Sources:** Heese IV: memory, imagination, inherited thought; Reed IV: genealogy and domestic intellectual activity.

**Original dynamic:** A household becomes inhabitable through the stories, arguments, and arrangements its members keep making together.
**Expression A:** He wants a home lively enough to think aloud in.
**Scene A:** A family debates where to put a new window over supper, inviting increasingly extravagant designs drawn in spilled flour.
**Expression B:** He feels protective curiosity toward an inheritance that may have been misunderstood.
**Scene B:** Behind a familiar ancestral portrait is a second household's name; he can ask an elder about it privately before deciding where the portrait belongs.

## V — Creativity

**Sources:** Heese V: verbal invention and flirtation; Reed V: dramatic expression and criticism.
Firmicus III.7.8 associates Mercury V with resource stewardship, while III.7.9 specifically makes evening-rising Mercury dissipate entrusted funds.
Those are reported outcomes, not supplied motives.

**Original dynamic:** The Prince can enjoy inventing an effect and deciding what resources to give it.
**Expression A:** He wants the pleasure of making someone laugh and answer in kind.
**Scene A:** A flirt proposes a poem whose next line must contradict the last; he can prolong their game without resolving its meaning.
**Expression B:** He wants a brief entertainment to be lavish because its extravagance delights him.
**Scene B:** Given the remaining feast purse, he considers spending it on an absurd puppet entrance, although simpler festivities are already arranged.

## VI — Labor

**Sources:** Heese VI: detailed skill and manual ingenuity; Reed VI: specialized knowledge.
Rough's practical creative-work reading explicitly adds Sun V; it cannot establish unconditioned Mercury VI.

**Original dynamic:** Necessary work becomes a problem the Prince can handle through technique, experimentation, and practical intelligence.
**Expression A:** He wants the satisfaction of making an awkward task work differently.
**Scene A:** A laundress keeps stopping to untangle the same pulley; he can help improvise a guide from objects already at hand.
**Expression B:** He is tired of having his expertise taken for granted and wants control over its use.
**Scene B:** A workshop expects him to demonstrate a faster stitch before discussing his place at the bench; he can bargain over the demonstration itself.

## VII — Relationships

**Sources:** Heese VII: discovering one's thinking through dialogue; Reed VII: mental rapport and competitive debate.

**Original dynamic:** A particular counterpart changes what the Prince can think, say, and agree to.
**Expression A:** He wants the exhilaration of letting another person's reasoning alter his position.
**Scene A:** Two prospective traveling companions discover that each has mistaken the other's reason for leaving, and can invent different terms for their journey.
**Expression B:** He wants the pleasure of winning this person's answer, even on an inconsequential question.
**Scene B:** His companion insists an old promise covers carrying both packs; the Prince sees a witty counterargument and can press it or enjoy being outplayed.

## VIII — Transformation

**Sources:** Heese VIII: investigative persuasion; Reed VIII: estates, disclosure, and secret keeping.

**Original dynamic:** After loss, access to information can change who must carry obligations and who controls what remains.
**Expression A:** He wants to understand a dead person's unfinished intention without pretending that knowledge removes grief.
**Scene A:** A sealed instruction names an unfamiliar creditor; the Prince can seek the story of that debt before distributing the remaining belongings.
**Expression B:** He wants influence over a settlement through knowledge others lack.
**Scene B:** Two heirs contest a shared boat, and he knows why its absent third owner still has a claim; disclosure can become part of his bargaining.

## IX — Pilgrimage

**Sources:** Heese IX: exchanging philosophies across cultures; Reed IX: study and travel.

**Original dynamic:** Distance exposes the Prince to different methods of explaining a world he thought he understood.
**Expression A:** He wants the delight of a question his own teachers never taught him to ask.
**Scene A:** Foreign navigators sort the stars by disappearance rather than position; he tries their method on a sky he knows.
**Expression B:** He wants to follow an unresolved disagreement across traditions.
**Scene B:** Two schools translate the same traveler's account differently, and he chooses which disputed passage to carry to a third reader.
The interest lies in reasoning and comparison; a single encompassing explanation need not arrive.

## XI — Friendship

**Sources:** Heese XI: intellectual friendships and changing aspirations; Reed XI: extensive contacts, networking, and possible social advantage.

**Original dynamic:** Ideas become possibilities through the particular people who receive, alter, and circulate them.
**Expression A:** He wants the excitement of an idea escaping its original circle.
**Scene A:** Friends sketch a night market that none could organize alone; he knows an unlikely acquaintance who would change what they are imagining.
**Expression B:** He enjoys being the person through whom interesting people first meet.
**Scene B:** Two competing invitation lists reach him before a gathering; he can arrange a surprising introduction or reserve it for a circle he prefers.
Neither expression requires universal mediation or public leadership.

## XII — The Hidden

**Sources:** Heese XII: discretion and symbolic thought; Reed XII: private thinking and selective disclosure.

**Original dynamic:** Where access is restricted, expression depends on who can hear it, how it travels, and whether it leaves a trace.
**Expression A:** He wants companionship through language that an overseeing authority cannot readily intercept.
**Scene A:** A confined correspondent sends a harmless-looking drawing through the laundry; he can answer its private joke in the same form.
**Expression B:** He wants to keep an unfinished thought his own without being recruited to explain it.
**Scene B:** During voluntary seclusion, a visitor asks to read the pages beneath his door; he can share one, invent a diversion, or leave them unseen.

## Independent overlap — Jupiter VI

**Sources:** Heese VI stresses ethical, useful work; Reed VI adds service and help from coworkers.
Rough VI wants work consonant with philosophy, with variety and fulfillment outweighing security.
Firmicus III.3.13–14 instead predicts hardship despite skilled occupations, with further conditions intensifying adversity or partly mitigating it through a waxing Moon's aspect without contrary planetary influence.
This is a historical outcome forecast, not evidence that someone wants hardship or deserves it.

**Original dynamic:** Necessary work can hold a hope for a more generous or meaningful life without guaranteeing improved circumstances.
**Expression A:** The Prince wants a larger range of useful work because repetitive security feels insufficient.
**Scene A:** A cook offers him a permanent place peeling roots; he proposes learning the whole kitchen, although the offer may not expand.
**Expression B:** He wants fellowship and plenty within an exhausting day, even while its unfairness remains unresolved.
**Scene B:** Skilled workers wait for delayed wages, and he brings out provisions for a shared meal that he is hungry to enjoy too.

## Gaps and useful disagreements

Modern overlap is strongest on communication, but sources vary between curiosity, profit, intimacy, and discretion.
Firmicus III broadens discovery beyond teaching; V supplies an alternative to exclusively playful modern interpretations, with its evening condition preserved.
Jupiter VI's historical adversity prevents an easy promise that meaningful service brings reward.
Rough's Mercury passages often require another Sun placement, limiting their use as independent Mercury evidence.
Birth predictions, medical claims, gender prescriptions, and causal explanations of imprisonment or bereavement are not adopted.
These are scene proposals, not evaluated player experiences, fixed drives, or completed encounter copy.
