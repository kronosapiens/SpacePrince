# Sun house research notes

Preliminary research for Sun in Houses I–IX and XI–XII, plus an independent Mars VII overlap.
The [full matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md) contains the lead synthesis after review; proposals here preserve the independent research stage.
Sources consulted 2026-10-04 without reading the house–planet matrix, pilot scenes, or other researchers' new notes.
The Prince's own natal Sun occupies these houses; rulership and planetary joy are separate matters.
The [house reference](../concept/HOUSE_MATRIX.md), [planet reference](../concept/PLANET_MATRIX.md), [Sun voice](../concept/PLANETS.md#3-sun), and [emotional-range guidance](../concept/DRIVES.md) supply the game framework.
Original dynamics, expressions, and scenes below are writing proposals, not natal predictions or approved encounter copy.

## I — Self

**Source:** Heese emphasizes presence and identification with one's outward image.

**Original dynamic:** Arriving as a particular embodied person can itself be an act the Prince enjoys choosing.
**Expressions:** He wants the exhilaration of taking up space; alternatively, he wants an altered appearance accepted without offering an account of what happened.

**Scene A:** A bath attendant offers to paint the travelers' marks on his skin with washable gold.
He can choose which to accent, leave himself undecorated, or inspect his whole reflection before answering.
**Scene B:** At a costume fitting, the available masks all make him look younger.
The maker has unpainted clay ready, and he can choose a face that resembles him now or enjoy an evening in the borrowed one.

## II — Livelihood

**Source:** Heese connects owned resources, security, and personal pride.

**Original dynamic:** Having something dependable at his disposal can make the Prince feel capable of directing his own life.
**Expressions:** He enjoys the substantial proof of an earned purse; alternatively, he dislikes needing a benefactor's permission to use something necessary.

**Scene A:** His payment will buy either sound traveling boots or the splendid clasp he has admired all week.
He can value usefulness or display without either purchase revealing his entire character.
**Scene B:** A patron offers use of an excellent tool but expects it returned before sunset.
The Prince can accept the restriction, buy a simpler tool outright, or keep working with his repaired one.

## III — Communication

**Source:** Heese emphasizes learning, mental agility, and communicating knowledge.

**Original dynamic:** The familiar world offers the Prince opportunities to become someone whose account is worth hearing.
**Expressions:** He delights in making a local explanation vivid; alternatively, he feels exposed when a sibling remembers an event differently.

**Scene A:** A new neighbor asks why the street changes its market signal halfway through the morning.
The Prince can explain the working custom, demonstrate it, or linger over the ridiculous incident that established it.
**Scene B:** His sister interrupts a favorite childhood story with a detail that changes its ending.
He can defend his recollection, invite her version, or tell both as competing family stories.

## IV — Home

**Source:** Heese stresses private foundations; Rough emphasizes understanding one's formative history.

**Original dynamic:** A household can give visible form to the life the Prince wants to regard as his own.
**Expressions:** He is proud to offer shelter on familiar terms; alternatively, he wants room for choices that family tradition cannot account for.

**Scene A:** Relatives bring the old household banner for his newly repaired front room.
He can hang it in the central place, find another place for it, or ask what each person remembers seeing beneath it.
**Scene B:** He opens his childhood sleeping room to a guest and discovers he still calls one corner forbidden.
The cupboard is empty now, and deciding what belongs there changes how he inhabits the room.

## V — Creativity

**Source:** Heese emphasizes distinctive expression, enjoyment, and being noticed.

**Original dynamic:** Making something allows the Prince to enjoy an unmistakable contribution without requiring an enduring achievement.
**Expressions:** He wants an audience's delighted response; alternatively, he wants to pursue an absurd invention simply because its possibility pleases him.

**Scene A:** A shadow-play troupe offers him the final figure in its evening performance.
He can make a grand entrance, invent an unexpected ending, or devise an effect that astonishes the performers too.
**Scene B:** Children invite him to add one rule to their game of balancing stones.
His rule can make it spectacular, ridiculous, or beautifully difficult, and he is welcome to play until the pile falls.

## VI — Labor

**Source:** Heese emphasizes work as identity; Reed emphasizes appreciation and meaningful work.

**Original dynamic:** A useful result can establish the Prince's competence even where another person controls the task.
**Expressions:** He takes satisfaction in getting an ordinary job right; alternatively, he resents having his skill treated as interchangeable labor.
The concern is what the work expresses, rather than how much strain he can endure.

**Scene A:** At a public kitchen, the supervisor wants loaves cut quickly, while the Prince knows a method that leaves each portion intact.
He can demonstrate it, use the required method, or ask to take responsibility for this batch.
**Scene B:** His injured hand prevents him from lifting sacks, but seeds still need sorting at a seated bench.
He can take that useful task or accept the offered rest without having to prove himself indispensable.

## VII — Relationships

**Source:** Heese stresses harmony; Reed allows dominance; Rough emphasizes self-discovery through equal counterparts.
Rough also questions an automatic preference for marriage.

**Original dynamic:** Choosing this particular counterpart helps the Prince discover what kind of shared life he wants to enter.
**Expressions:** He feels proud to join someone he admires; alternatively, he finds that a valued partnership now asks him to remain someone he no longer wants to be.

**Scene A:** An accomplished craftswoman offers him a share in her workshop and asks what he would make there.
Admiring her work does not yet answer what he wants their partnership to contain.
**Scene B:** A longtime associate proposes renewing their trading agreement with the same division of roles.
The Prince can renew it, request another role, or end the arrangement while acknowledging what they accomplished together.

## VIII — Transformation

**Source:** Heese stresses depth and intense experience; Rough emphasizes vulnerability and accommodating others.
Reed includes stewardship of inheritances; Houlding supplies death, loss, and shared obligations.

**Original dynamic:** An ending reveals how much of the Prince's apparent independence rested on another person's presence or resources.
**Expressions:** He wants the complete truth about what supported him; alternatively, he wants to settle what remains without converting the death into an explanation of himself.

**Scene A:** A deceased patron's ledger lists an old gift as a loan, while an attached letter suggests affection.
The executor offers to examine the account with him before its immediate settlement.
**Scene B:** At a companion's funeral, the officiant invites him to describe what the death has changed.
He can describe an actual change, name what has simply been lost, or decline to make his grief into a public account.

## IX — Pilgrimage

**Source:** Heese emphasizes knowledge and ideals; Rough emphasizes finding a place within a larger order.

**Original dynamic:** A distant practice can make the Prince's convictions something he must actually act from.
**Expressions:** He enjoys discovering a belief he can affirm wholeheartedly; alternatively, he wants to defend an existing conviction even when his hosts find it puzzling.

**Scene A:** A distant observatory invites visitors to record what they believe a recurring light signifies before hearing competing explanations.
He can offer his account, test it against the records, or leave the question open.
**Scene B:** A pilgrimage oath contains one promise he cannot endorse.
The keeper can explain its meaning, accept a differently worded promise, or let him visit without joining the observance.

## XI — Friendship

**Source:** Heese connects identity with a diverse circle and shared hopes.

**Original dynamic:** The Prince can recognize himself in what a group wants to make possible together.
**Expressions:** He takes pride in belonging to this particular company; alternatively, he feels that an emerging shared ambition no longer represents why he joined.
Neither requires a title or public office.

**Scene A:** Friends planning an evening school disagree about opening its limited seats to strangers or keeping them for current members.
He can argue for the version he wants to help create, reconsider the group undertaking, or offer a workable compromise.
**Scene B:** His old companions celebrate a failed expedition because they are glad they attempted it together.
He can enjoy being one of them, describe what the attempt meant to him, or admit that he no longer wants another.

## XII — The Hidden

**Source:** Heese emphasizes seclusion; Reed permits purposeful work outside public view.
Houlding supplies actual exclusion and restricted access.

**Original dynamic:** Reduced visibility changes what the Prince can do without erasing his ability to choose a purpose.
**Expressions:** He welcomes freedom from presenting himself; alternatively, he resents having his life reduced to what an institution permits others to see.

**Scene A:** A secluded retreat asks guests to put away their insignia and keep silence until morning.
He can enter as an anonymous guest, ask what the practice permits, or decline its terms.
**Scene B:** A hostel outside the city houses exiles forbidden to enter for tonight's celebration.
Inside, the Prince can begin the account of his journey, join the residents' supper, or keep an evening entirely to himself.

## Independent overlap — Mars VII

**Source:** Heese emphasizes challenge and discovering assertiveness through another person.
Reed emphasizes actively pursuing partnerships; Rough instead describes recruiting a forceful partner to advance one's interests.
Rough's claim of house-based detriment is excluded.

**Original dynamic:** Another person's resistance gives the Prince's effort a concrete object and makes the terms of cooperation matter.
**Expressions:** He enjoys a counterpart who pushes back; alternatively, he wants an ally to conduct a confrontation he would prefer to avoid.

**Scene A:** A partner rehearses a demand for the return of their lodging deposit, then asks who will speak to the proprietor.
The Prince can make the demand, support his partner's case, or agree on a revised amount before entering.
**Scene B:** A rival offers a joint repair contract but challenges his proposed division of the difficult work.
He can defend his terms, propose a direct demonstration of each person's method, or withdraw the offer while the agreement remains unsigned.

## Gaps and editorial limits

Sun VIII needs an explicit editorial bridge from psychological depth to material loss; the primary Cafe passage alone does not establish estate or bereavement meanings.
Rough's private Sun IV offers a useful check against making every solar concern public acclaim.
VII's readings diverge on accommodation and dominance; the proposed pairing concerns selfhood through relationship, while Mars concerns exertion and challenge.

The adaptations omit automatic outcomes, health predictions, gender roles, karmic blame, and equations between houses and signs.
Affection, pride, frustration, refusal, and pleasure remain available without a corrective ending.
No source passage establishes that these scenes will engage players; writing and playtesting must assess that separately.
