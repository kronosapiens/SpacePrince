## I — Self

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes immediate involvement, independence, and spontaneous initiative.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want his first act to establish that he is participating on his own terms.
One expression is delight in beginning before a role has been assigned.
Another is impatience with the image others have prepared for him, even when it is flattering.
The bodily arrival matters more than eventual status.

## II — Livelihood

**Source notes:** Heese combines earning, appetite, and possession.
Reed adds financial competition and independent enterprise.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want a resource because acquiring it gives him usable power of his own.
One expression is satisfaction in securing provisions without another person's permission.
Another is a sharp wish to keep what he obtained, even when lending it would be easy.
Possessiveness need not be disguised as prudent stewardship.

## III — Communication

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes direct expression, provocative debate, and animated exchanges.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want an exchange with someone familiar to become an active encounter rather than something he merely receives.
One expression is the fun of a brisk argument both participants understand as play.
Another is annoyance that someone else keeps supplying the version of events everyone repeats.
Local relationships and circulating words give this its setting.

## IV — Home

**Source notes:** Heese connects domestic initiative, family protection, and governing the household.
Reed emphasizes control and family conflict.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want the place where he lives to answer to his present choices.
One expression is eagerness to make a neglected room usable.
Another is a wish to prevail over a family habit whose persistence feels like someone else's authority.
Protection is possible, but the disagreement may simply concern competing domestic preferences.

## V — Creativity

**Source notes:** Heese describes pursuit of pleasure, risk, games, and exciting projects.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want the moment when desire turns into an attempt.
One expression is exhilaration at a playful risk without useful consequences.
Another is the appetite to be the person who receives a particular invitation or wins a particular game.
The writing can let success be sweet and losing sting without administering a lesson.

## VI — Labor

**Source notes:** Heese stresses independent methods and impatience with delay or interference.
Reed emphasizes demanding work standards.
Houlding includes subordinate labor and bodily vulnerability.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want command of the method by which necessary work gets finished.
One expression is satisfaction in finding a pace that moves the task along.
Another is competitive irritation when somebody else completes it differently or receives easier treatment.
Neither endurance nor refusal is automatically the right response.

## VII — Relationships

**Source notes:** Heese explicitly values challenging partners.
Reed adds active pursuit and determination within partnerships.

**Original adaptation:** Another person's resistance can make partnership more desirable to the Prince.
One expression is excitement at meeting someone who can answer him with equal force.
Another is wanting to secure a shared decision before hesitation dissolves the opportunity.
The other person has their own will; welcomed challenge does not require mistreatment or eventual reconciliation.

## VIII — Transformation

**Source notes:** Heese discusses threatened trust and concentrated pursuit.
Reed names conflict over joint finances and inheritances.
Houlding includes loss, others' assets, and debts.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want a decisive act where another person's claim or an irreversible absence limits what he controls.
One expression is urgency to settle a shared obligation and regain freedom.
Another is anger that wanting an inherited object strongly cannot make it wholly his.
Action need not undo loss or produce growth.

## IX — Pilgrimage

**Source notes:** Heese connects adventure, debate, and learning through challenge.
Reed adds promoting convictions while traveling.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want a belief or distant possibility to meet an actual trial.
One expression is eagerness to attempt an unfamiliar practice.
Another is the desire to defend his conviction before people who do not grant it authority.
The location or teaching must be genuinely outside his familiar frame.
Neither persuasion nor conversion is guaranteed.

## XI — Friendship

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes helpful initiative, causes, and energetic friends.
Reed includes group leadership and power struggles.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want to turn a shared wish into something his friends can actually begin together.
One expression is contagious eagerness to set a collective plan in motion.
Another is rivalry over who supplies its direction, even among people he likes.
The undertaking belongs to the circle; public office and institutional authority are unnecessary.

## XII — The Hidden

**Source notes:** Heese describes resourcefulness alongside inhibited assertion.
Reed allows deliberate activity outside scrutiny.
Houlding includes confinement and exclusion.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want room to act when ordinary access or observation constrains him.
One expression is absorbed satisfaction in pursuing a private undertaking.
Another is frustrated urgency to make contact with a person kept outside ordinary life.
The circumstances must establish concealment or restriction, rather than treating every unspoken desire as Twelfth-house material.

## Independent overlap — Sun VI

**Source notes:** Heese connects work with identity and warns against dependence on appreciation.
Reed directly recommends praise as motivation.
These are different emphases rather than a settled dispute.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want ordinary necessary work to bear something recognizably his.
One expression is quiet pride in a competent contribution.
Another is wanting a supervisor or recipient to notice effort that disappears into daily provision.
He need not transcend that wish for acknowledgement.
Compared with Mars VI's control of method, this proposal centers recognition of oneself in the work.

## Gaps

VIII requires the strongest editorial joining of source themes: practical shared claims constrain the Prince's action, beyond either author's account of intensity.
XII's chosen privacy and imposed exclusion remain distinct situations.
The sources' repeated corrective advice is not a requirement to make play reward moderation.
Historical delineations, economic costs, and actual player experience remain outside this note's evidence.
