# House and planet blind scene exercise

A separate writer received only the named house and central dynamic for each assignment, with no research notes, existing scenes, or reviewer comments.
These 38 seeds preserve the three continuation rounds, including a second test of the corrected Mars VII dynamic.
The [research review](HOUSE_PLANET_REVIEW.md#blind-scene-exercise) evaluates both useful results and cases of repetition or house drift.
These are raw authoring exercises, not replacements for the [matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md) or approved encounter copy.

The instructions asked for two roughly forty-to-sixty-word scenes per dynamic, with no mechanics, compulsory lesson, or required downside.
Each round's final uncertainty note also comes from the blind writer.

## Round 1: Moon and Venus

### Moon IV

#### A

At the boardinghouse, everyone eats from the same pot but returns to a separate room before the kettle boils.
The Prince keeps finding reasons to linger at the table.
Tonight the landlady asks whether she should lay his breakfast upstairs, where he can have some peace.

#### B

His aunt has kept his childhood room exactly as it was, including the rule that someone checks his blankets before bed.
The Prince has begun waiting for that knock.
When she apologizes for treating him like a child, her hand already rests on the folded spare quilt.

### Moon VI

#### A

The Prince trims the observatory lamps each evening, and the astronomers have begun leaving supper beside his tools.
Now they knock at his bedroom whenever a flame sputters.
Tonight he hears a familiar voice outside while someone downstairs carries his untouched plate back to the kitchen.

#### B

The ferry cook has learned the dough the Prince usually kneads, freeing him to spend mornings ashore.
Returning early, he finds everyone eating happily without him.
The cook sets out his apron for tomorrow and asks which days she should stop expecting him.

### Moon VIII

#### A

After the gardener dies, the Prince discovers that every planting date was kept in her head.
The beds outside his window have always told him what month it is.
Her son arrives to collect her tools, including the wooden dibber still standing beside an unfinished row.

#### B

The Prince's elder sister is leaving the house after selling the land that supported them both.
She has packed the household ledger but left her chair by the stove.
He asks about the chair; she answers with arrangements for his journey, assuming he intends to follow.

### Venus III

#### A

The tea seller used to laugh before the Prince finished describing his journeys.
Lately she smiles and reaches for another cup.
He has brought her a magnificent story, but she is talking about the stray dog that finally slept beneath her counter, and keeps glancing toward its empty blanket.

#### B

His brother's daily messages once began with an insult and ended with the weather.
Since the brother married, the Prince has answered with stiff congratulations on every small domestic success.
This morning a message arrives describing burnt toast, followed by a long silence where their usual teasing would fit.

### Venus VIII

#### A

At the division of her belongings, the Prince's former companion places their silver cups in his pile and keeps the chipped bowl they used every morning.
Everyone calls the arrangement generous.
She reaches for the next box while he turns one untouched cup around by its stem.

#### B

The sculptor's heirs offer the Prince any finished work from her studio to settle his share in its contents.
He keeps looking at the plaster cast of his hands, which they have marked for disposal.
Her daughter says he can take it too, if he helps clear the rubbish.

### Venus XII

#### A

The Prince's married lover has found a room above a shuttered shop where they can spend an afternoon together.
It contains a bed, a washstand, and nowhere to sit over tea.
She laughs at his suggestion of bringing chairs, then asks whether he really means to carry them there himself.

#### B

During the court's month of mourning, the Prince discovers that the linen cupboard muffles music almost completely.
He takes a small flute there after supper.
Tonight the laundress is already inside, tapping her foot to a tune he played yesterday, with a stool placed beside her own.

### Uncertainty

The dynamics leave the causes of loss, the terms of material settlement, and the reasons for secrecy open.
These seeds supply different circumstances without assuming those circumstances belong in the dynamics themselves.

## Round 2: Sun and Mars

Mars VII in this round used the earlier wording about a counterpart whose resistance gives the Prince's desire something definite to meet.
Round three tests the broadened dynamic, which also admits a forceful ally.

### Sun II

#### A

The Prince's clocks have all begun striking correctly after months of repair.
A visitor offers to arrange them in a handsome glass cabinet, with the Prince's tools displayed beneath.
He is still carrying the smallest clock from room to room, delighted each time it tells him the hour.

#### B

Before opening his collection to visitors, the Prince finds his steward removing a cracked bowl from the shelves.
He discovered it years ago beneath a dealer's table and recognized the potter's hand before anyone else did.
The steward has left a conspicuous empty space among the celebrated pieces.

### Sun VI

#### A

The Prince has taken over making soup for the boatmen's midday meal.
Today he has brought herbs from his own garden and a second ladle so he can taste as he goes.
The first boatman through the door lifts the lid and asks what smells different.

#### B

Every evening the Prince records the reservoir level and the condition of its banks.
His little drawings now distinguish each stretch of shore better than the official names do.
A new keeper has brought clean ledgers with printed boxes and is waiting to copy across the last week's entries.

### Sun VIII

#### A

After his mother's death, the Prince opens the workshop accounts she kept for him.
Beside each triumphant commission is a note showing which supplier she persuaded to wait.
A supplier arrives carrying timber for the next job and asks whom he should speak to now.

#### B

His partner has left the travelling theatre, taking the habit of answering every uncertain question backstage.
The Prince has always called himself a director who gives performers freedom.
At rehearsal, three actors offer incompatible ways to end a scene and wait for him to say which one they should try.

### Mars IV

#### A

The Prince has dragged his bed toward the room's only sunny window.
His housemate comes home carrying a reading chair intended for that exact spot.
They stand on opposite sides of the bed, both smiling from the effort of hauling furniture, while the last light moves across the floor.

#### B

While the Prince is away, his family removes the bolt from his bedroom door so everyone can reach the balcony.
He returns as a cousin carries a tray through the room.
The bolt lies on his desk beside its screws, and the cousin asks him to hold the door.

### Mars VII

#### A

The Prince's dancing partner refuses to follow the turn he keeps beginning.
She shows him the direction she wants instead, then takes his hand again.
The musicians restart the phrase, and he realizes he has been waiting all evening for someone to pull back this hard.

#### B

The Prince wants to take the boat through the narrow channel; his companion plants an oar against the jetty and names the longer route.
She has a reason for every objection he raises.
She lifts the spare oar toward him and waits, her own still braced against the wood.

### Mars XI

#### A

For weeks the neighbours have talked about clearing the overgrown courtyard for dancing.
Tonight the Prince arrives with a rake and finds three others holding tools, grinning at one another through the locked gate.
The caretaker opens it and asks where they would like the first pile of cuttings.

#### B

The Prince and his friends have finally agreed to stage their ridiculous play in the square.
Now everyone is proposing costumes, and nobody has said the opening line aloud.
He climbs onto the fountain's dry rim with the script; one friend immediately scrambles up beside him, demanding a part.

### Uncertainty

The dynamics do not specify whether possessions should be useful or expressive, whether a counterpart is welcome, or what the group intends.
The pairs explore different answers without treating any one answer as required.

## Round 3: Mercury and Jupiter; revised Mars VII

Jupiter XII used the earlier dynamic, “The Prince wants room for hope when help is neither visible nor assured.”
The final editorial review subsequently broadened it to include giving as well as awaiting help.

### Mercury IX — Pilgrimage

#### A

The mountain guides give every direction as uphill or downhill, even inside a room.
The Prince has begun speaking their way and keeps catching distinctions his own language misses.
Before he leaves for the flat coast, a guide asks how he will describe the house they are sitting in.

#### B

In the village court, an obligation can begin with someone who could have helped but did nothing.
The Prince has followed three judgments using this rule.
Now the clerk invites him to examine a dispute in which both neighbours claim they were waiting for the other to ask.

### Mercury XII — The Hidden

#### A

The censor allows the Prince to send household instructions to his sister but strikes out every mention of their quarrel.
He wants her to know she can come home without making that invitation available to their father.
The next letter lies open beside a list of rooms to prepare for winter.

#### B

The council forbids the Prince to speak about the succession, but has asked him to choose a story for the public festival.
He finds one whose ending says almost exactly what he means.
The storyteller wants permission to improvise, and is already explaining her version to the musicians.

### Jupiter I — Self

#### A

The Prince arrives at the inn with rain in his sleeves and enough baggage for a single night.
Tomorrow's coach will take him wherever he names.
Over supper, a fellow traveller asks what brings him this far, and he discovers he wants to describe the journey as an expedition.

#### B

At the scholars' gathering, the Prince is assigned a stool near the doors because he has no position or sponsor.
Before the first lecture, someone asks what he hopes to hear.
He begins describing a school he would like to establish, spreading his notes across two empty chairs.

### Jupiter VI — Labor

#### A

The Prince has made the kitchen run smoothly enough that the steward praises him before the whole household.
His request for one free evening is still refused; dinner must be served.
A friend arrives with the part they saved for him in their play and asks whether to keep waiting.

#### B

The ferry crew gladly covers the Prince's duties whenever it can, but winter leaves everyone working until dark.
He wants to begin a history of the river towns while the old pilots are alive.
Tonight a retired pilot sits beside the landing, ready to talk, as another ferry signals for repairs.

### Jupiter VIII — Transformation

#### A

Their uncle's death leaves the Prince and his cousin jointly responsible for a small theatre that has been closed for years.
His cousin proposes clearing it for storage.
The Prince opens a cupboard full of masks and begins naming everyone he knows who might come there to perform.

#### B

After the expedition leader dies, its maps and remaining supplies pass to a committee that includes the Prince.
Most members want to catalogue everything and return home.
He has found the leader's unfinished route through the islands, and the quartermaster asks which crates she should begin unloading.

### Jupiter XII — The Hidden

#### A

No answer has come to the Prince's appeal, and the prison clerk cannot tell him whether it was delivered.
He has been asked to name someone who might receive him if he is released.
The form waits beneath his hand while he thinks of people he has not seen for years.

#### B

The Prince has spent the winter in a remote hospice, with no visitors and no promise that travelling will become possible again.
The gardener offers him a patch of ground to use as he pleases.
He asks for seeds of something that will flower after the summer solstice.

### Mars VII — Relationships

#### A

The Prince means to swim as far as the buoy and return before breakfast.
His companion dives in first, turns, and calls that she is going to the opposite bank.
He can see the distance clearly from the steps; he finds himself studying where he would come ashore beside her.

#### B

The Prince has prepared a modest request to reopen the public gardens.
His neighbour reads it, strikes the word modest from his introduction, and insists on coming to the meeting.
As she rehearses the objections they will face, he begins adding the locked glasshouses to what he wants reopened.

### Uncertainty

The dynamics leave the unfamiliar system, the source of expressive restrictions, and the scale of the Prince's larger purposes unspecified.
They also leave open whether hope concerns outside help or a future he can still imagine, and whether another person's force attracts him, challenges him, or supports him.
