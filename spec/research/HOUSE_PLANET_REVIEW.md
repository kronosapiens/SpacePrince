Research for [issue #9](https://github.com/kronosapiens/SpacePrince/issues/9), completed 2026-10-04.
The [full matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md) brings together all 84 placements, 168 scene seeds, and 18 selected sign variations.
The [pilot review](HOUSE_PLANET_PILOT_REVIEW.md) preserves the first eighteen entries' process and findings; this report covers the remaining 66 and the complete reference.
The comparison baseline is repository commit `bbb07bb08162173910c33d52a072af86aaa906e3`.

The user authorized completing all 84 before the next user review, with internal research and review rounds.

It does not implement encounters, prices, rewards, eligibility, or gameplay mechanics.

## Method and independence

Two researchers worked separately through Moon/Venus, Sun/Mars, and Mercury/Jupiter.
They established source readings before seeing the existing matrix, the pilot's scenes, the lead's drafts, or each other's proposals.
Each assignment included a placement also studied by the other researcher.
The overlap compared interpretations, not just the number of agreeing websites.

| Round | New placements | Independently researched overlaps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | Saturn I–XII; other six planets X | Saturn X |
| Moon and Venus | Both planets I–IX, XI–XII | Moon IV; Venus VIII |
| Sun and Mars | Both planets I–IX, XI–XII | Sun VI; Mars VII |
| Mercury and Jupiter | Both planets I–IX, XI–XII | Mercury IX; Jupiter VI |

A separate historical researcher inspected ten direct passages or passage groups from Firmicus and Valens.
The lead inspected the contemporary passages used, checked the historical comparisons, and wrote the synthesis across assignments.
A separate reviewer then checked each continuation round against the sources and earlier entries.
That reviewer had written the historical notes, so review of those notes was not independent of their author; the lead supplied the additional passage check.
A fresh editorial reviewer examined the consolidated 84 entries and sign variations across both axes.

The [research notes linked from the matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md#source-basis) retain source metadata, locators, differences, conditions, and preliminary proposals.
They are evidence of the research stage; the matrix holds the subsequent synthesis.
The old pilot document now points to its entries in that single reference.
The original pilot remains available in commit `b76199d`.

## What the research changed

The useful question became what a particular want means to the Prince in this situation.
Recognition can provide authorship, personal acceptance, authority to act, or the pleasure of admiration.
It need not identify a good person or lead him to become one.

| Placement | Baseline scene | Revised direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moon IV | Receive care in a familiar bowl | Accept the old sleeping place or want an unfamiliar room in a loved household: belonging can require a changed arrangement. |
| Venus III | Add a caring greeting to a message | Want a familiar person to enjoy hearing from him: attention, flirtation, and vanity can accompany affection. |
| Sun VI | Choose a place in the washing line | Want a cook to notice skill that disappears into daily provision: competence and recognition differ from control of the work. |
| Mars VII | Ask a companion to divide a burden again | Enjoy a challenging counterpart or recruit a forceful ally: assertiveness need not mean personally conducting every confrontation. |
| Venus VIII | Help mourners divide a scarf | Want his own share to acknowledge a particular attachment: material settlement and emotional reciprocity need not coincide. |
| Mercury VIII | Clarify an heir's accounts | Seek information or use a receipt to strengthen his own claim: understanding need not make him an impartial mediator. |
| Jupiter VI | Learn the full work process | Want variety or a shared meal despite overdue wages: fellowship need not repair unjust conditions. |
| Jupiter XII | Help an isolated person imagine travel | Decide whether to maintain an observance after a visitor cannot come, or want gratitude for a concealed gift: hope and generosity can remain disappointed or interested. |

The independent overlaps contributed differences worth preserving.
Moon IV can seek continuity or a more fitting domestic arrangement.
Venus VIII can seek reciprocity or remain attached without receiving it.
Sun VI can enjoy competence or need another worker's acknowledgment.
Mars VII can welcome opposition or enlist somebody else's force.
Mercury IX explores how an unfamiliar account works; Jupiter IX asks what it would mean to live by it.
Jupiter VI can want fulfilling work without expecting work to become fair.

These are original game distinctions informed by the readings, not exclusive planetary territories established by those readings.
The [drive vocabulary](../concept/DRIVES.md) remains provisional.
Phrases such as wanting an answer, keeping a remnant, being desired, or enjoying competence often give a writer more than assigning a broad category first.
No new drive taxonomy was imposed.

## Source findings and limits

Cafe Astrology and Theresa Reed provide most of the contemporary placement coverage outside Saturn.
Suzanne Rough supplies useful alternatives; Houlding supplies house boundaries rather than direct psychological placement readings.
AstroLibrary's script credit does not establish independent authorship or an independent tradition.
Repeated themes across these pages should not be counted as consensus.
The notes distinguish Cafe's signed primary material from separately credited report inserts.

The [historical comparisons](HOUSE_PLANET_TRADITIONAL_NOTES.md) changed some authoring questions without replacing modern psychology with ancient predictions.
Firmicus's Mercury V contrast retains its phase condition; its spending outcome does not establish a motive for extravagant play.
Sun V permits a comparison involving friends' help, and Mars XI a favorable outcome, without making either scene a reward for virtue.
Jupiter VI challenges the expectation that skilled, useful work secures better circumstances.
Valens's adverse judgment of benefics in XII remains in tension with contemporary promises of hidden protection.
The resulting scenes permit hope without promising rescue.

The historical notes identify translations, book sections, printed/PDF page distinctions, and direct links.
Firmicus's missing Moon V–VIII material and Valens's conditional rulership/aspect passages limit what can be claimed as simple occupancy evidence.
These ten comparisons are a small historical sample, not traditional coverage of every placement or a survey of all schools.
No causal personality claims, medical forecasts, inherited social rankings, or house–sign dignity substitutions were adopted.

The Mercury/Jupiter source review found no necessary correction.
The fresh full-matrix editorial review subsequently found three narrower dynamics, recorded below.

Their other scenes and stated motives distinguish them, but a later author should avoid repeatedly selecting the similar seeds together.
Mercury VIII could more explicitly name using or disclosing knowledge as well as having access to it.
Venus in Aquarius in X remains a comparatively thin sign example, as already noted in the pilot.

The fresh reviewer read every entry, compared all seven planets in each house, followed each planet across twelve houses, and checked all eighteen sign variations against the existing sign reference.
This was an independent editorial review of the adaptations, not another complete source-verification pass.

| Necessary finding | Applied revision |

## Blind scene exercise

A separate writer received only eighteen selected central dynamics and house names, with no existing scenes, expressions, research notes, or reviewer comments.
Three rounds produced 36 seeds; a second test of the revised Mars VII dynamic added two more.
The [complete 38-seed exercise](HOUSE_PLANET_SCENE_EXERCISE.md) preserves the outputs and the writer's uncertainties.
The assessments below belong to the lead editor.

| Placement | Fresh result | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moon IV | A landlady offers breakfast alone after everyone else has left. | Useful variation in the availability of domestic company; the other output repeated the prepared-childhood-bed motif. |
| Moon VI | Travelers are content while the ferry cook is absent, then ask when he will return. | Opens the wish to be needed alongside relief; the second output again centered a caregiver's availability. |
| Venus VIII | A former companion offers valuable cups but keeps the chipped bowl they used together. | Strong distinction between settlement value and acknowledgment of attachment; material generosity need not satisfy him. |
| Venus XII | A private lover's room has a bed but no chairs, and the Prince wants somewhere to take tea. | Ordinary shared pleasure gives secrecy a different emotional texture without demanding punishment or redemption. |
| Sun VI | His drawings distinguish reservoir banks better than official names; a replacement brings a standardized ledger. | His mark on necessary work can matter beyond applause or superior efficiency. |
| Sun VIII | His mother's accounts reveal the supplier credit that sustained his apparent independence. | Loss changes the material support as well as his self-understanding; the other theatre-partner seed needs clearer shared-property stakes to secure VIII rather than VII. |
| Mars IV | A housemate's reading chair and his bed both claim the sunny window. | A domestic contest can be playful and bodily, without a wrongdoer or a repair that helps everyone. |
| Mars XI | Friends arrive with tools for the courtyard he wanted to clear. | Valid uncomplicated satisfaction, but close to the matrix's playing-ground scene: dynamics alone do not prevent repeated imagery. |
| Mercury IX | He tries to translate uphill/downhill directions for a flat coastal world. | Productive change of explanatory system; the alternative village-court seed needs an explicit distant learning setting to keep IX clear. |
| Mercury XII | A permitted household letter carries an invitation a censor must not recognize. | Different purpose for indirect language; the festival-story alternative risks drifting toward public office unless the restriction remains central. |
| Jupiter VI | A saved part in a play waits while a steward refuses time away from work. | The larger life can be outside the task; the lost opportunity is not compensated by praise. |
| Jupiter VIII | He wants to reopen an inherited theatre that a co-heir would use for storage. | Broader purpose becomes a concrete disagreement over resources after death. |
| Jupiter XII | A secluded hospice resident plants something that will flower after midsummer. | Hope can concern an imaginable future without an arriving helper; this stretches the dynamic usefully without promising recovery. |
| Mars VII, revised | A neighbor strengthens his petition and insists on accompanying him. | The new prompt admits an ally; earlier outputs concentrated on resisting counterparts, including another boat-route dispute. |

The exercise tests whether the reference can generate writing, not whether players will feel the represented emotions.
Jupiter XII's two blind outputs both explored the earlier prompt's hope for oneself; they do not test the later wording about giving.
The writer's uncertainty also exposed where a dynamic needs the house situation beside it: unfamiliarity alone does not establish Pilgrimage, and separation alone does not establish Transformation.
No blind output was automatically promoted to encounter copy.

## Assessment and next use

The method earned its extra work where separate readings disagreed, where a second scene exposed an incomplete dynamic, and where source conditions prevented an attractive but false generalization.
The blind exercise was most useful as a way to discover repetition and unclear house stakes.
It supplies evidence for revising guidance, not an objective measure of literary quality.

The full matrix permits attachment, envy, vanity, possession, strategic disclosure, resentment, relief, and pleasure alongside care and endurance.
It does not require a negative counterpart for every enjoyable act.
The Prince can want several things at once, and other people can reasonably want something different.

Coverage is complete; historical depth and authorial variety remain uneven.
Saturn benefited from a dedicated contemporary series, while the other planets lean heavily on a small shared source pool.
Some central wants remain narrow, and repeated settings reveal a limited stock of props and institutions.
Jupiter X's self-interested patronage remains a deliberately identified extension with thinner direct support.
The sign examples combine existing references; they are not new research into eighteen three-factor placements.

The next useful step is user review of the complete reference, followed by selecting varied scenes for encounter design and playtesting.
That later work can test whether contrasting motives produce interesting choices and whether the player feels anything beyond the Prince's stated concern.
Research alone cannot answer those questions.
