# Historical planet-in-house passage checks

Consulted 2026-10-04 for the all-84 house–planet research pass.
These ten checks offer historical comparisons for Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, and Jupiter.
They supplement the contemporary readings; they do not establish consensus or validate astrological predictions.
Compare them with [Drives](../concept/DRIVES.md), the [house reference](../concept/HOUSE_MATRIX.md), and the [planet frameworks](../concept/PLANET_MATRIX.md#planet-frameworks).

**Passage** below is a deliberately short paraphrase of the historical text.
**Authoring question** is original editorial analysis, not the historical author's account of a person's motives.
The questions test the project's present emphasis on interior experience and chosen agency.
No passage by itself demonstrates that a Prince wants the outcome it predicts, deserves that outcome, or has a corresponding moral personality.

## Editions actually inspected

- **Firmicus Maternus, *Matheseos libri VIII*, Book III**, in Jean Rhys Bram's *Ancient Astrology: Theory and Practice* (Noyes Press, 1975).
  The [complete translation scan](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf) was downloaded and its relevant passages read directly.
  Printed page numbers below belong to Bram; PDF pages are four higher in this scan.
  The [Latin Book III](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Firmicus_Maternus/Mathesis/3%2A.html), Kroll–Skutsch's Teubner text, was also inspected for chapter structure and selected corresponding passages.
  This is not a complete independent translation check.
- **Vettius Valens, *Anthologies*, Book II**, Mark T. Riley's preliminary translation, reformatted and annotated by Jane Griscti.
  The [edition description](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/valens_janegca.html) identifies Riley's translation and links Griscti's repository.
  The [complete PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/Valens-Anthologies-Annotated.pdf) and the linked chapter files were read directly.
  Page numbers below refer to this reformatted PDF, not the 2022 commercial edition or Riley's original pagination.
  Its planet glyphs extract poorly as plain text; the chapter files retain explicit planet names.

These are direct readings of translations of historical works, not ancient passages reconstructed from a contemporary interpretation.
Neither Greek manuscript work nor a comparison of all available English translations was undertaken.

## Ten useful comparisons

### Moon in III

**Passage:** Moon here, assigned the Ascendant or Lot and in its proper face, signifies authority over cities and treasuries.
[Valens II.13, opening paragraph, printed p. 108](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/book02/13-3rd.tex).

**Authoring question:** Can the Moon's intimate register coexist with being entrusted with something publicly consequential?
The research should leave room for ambition, pride, dependence, and reluctance without claiming any is contained in this forecast.
A purely conversational or nostalgic entry would miss this possible contrast.
The additional conditions belong in the source note wherever this passage is reused.

### Moon in VIII

**Passage:** The waxing Moon is the stated exception to this chapter's unfavorable evaluation of planets in VIII.
[Valens II.8, final sentence, printed p. 103](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/book02/08-8th.tex).

**Authoring question:** Does an eighth-house entry permit composure, practical concern, or relief, as well as emotional intensity?
This is a useful challenge to a uniform account of crisis, but it supplies very little positive content by itself.
Do not invent an ancient doctrine of therapeutic transformation to fill the gap.
Nor does this sentence relocate the Moon's conventional planetary joy from III to VIII.

### Mercury in III

**Passage:** Firmicus names ritual and healing practitioners, astrologers, and people making discoveries beyond received teaching.
[Firmicus III.7.6, printed p. 100 / PDF p. 104](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=104).

**Authoring question:** What makes knowledge credible, and who has permission to use it?
The entry could accommodate curiosity, professional pride, rivalry, or a wish to be useful.
Those motives would be ours to develop.
Avoid flattening Mercury into general cleverness or treating every third-house encounter as an exchange of messages.

### Mercury in V

**Passage:** Resource management and accumulation contrast with dissipation when Mercury is an evening star.
[Firmicus III.7.8–9, printed p. 100 / PDF p. 104](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=104).

**Authoring question:** Can pleasure or making something include a serious desire to keep, spend, or control resources?
This could broaden a playful Mercury entry without making calculation inherently joyless.
Retain the phase distinction in attribution; a writer may explore both possibilities, but cannot present them as Firmicus's unconditional personality sketch.

### Sun in V

**Passage:** Success and fulfilled wishes come substantially through friends' help.
[Firmicus III.5.18, opening sentences, printed p. 90 / PDF p. 94](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=94).

**Authoring question:** What does recognition feel like when one's achievement depends on other people?
Gratitude, delight, entitlement, and embarrassment are distinct possibilities for the Prince.
The passage helps question an exclusively solitary account of creative self-expression.
It does not require a lesson about sharing credit or imply that enjoyment must conceal vanity.

### Sun in IX

**Passage:** Making divine images, temples, and hymns can bring public distinction.
[Firmicus III.5.33, printed p. 93 / PDF p. 97](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=97).

**Authoring question:** What might the Prince want from giving a belief a visible form?
Devotion, pleasure in beauty, recognition, and belonging need not coincide.
This direction would make a ninth-house concern tangible while preserving the difference between believing something and being seen to represent it.
It does not establish that the historical subject was inwardly sincere or insincere.

### Venus in XI

**Passage:** Morning Venus receiving the Moon indicates early authority and powerful friendships; Mercury strengthens this prediction.
[Firmicus III.6.24, printed p. 97 / PDF p. 101](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=101).

**Authoring question:** Can affection include wanting admission, influence, or protection?
This supports testing a wider range than easy sociability and shared ideals.
Neither opportunism nor selfless loyalty follows automatically.
Keep the astronomical conditions attached to the evidence; the proposed emotional possibilities belong to the game, and the prediction does not become a gameplay advantage.

### Mars in XI

**Passage:** The opening judgment promises increased income and popularity.
[Firmicus III.4.33, printed p. 87 / PDF p. 91](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=91).

**Authoring question:** Could the Prince enjoy being effective and welcomed, without the group first becoming an obstacle?
An entry can allow uncomplicated satisfaction alongside harder possibilities elsewhere.
We should not quietly rewrite every favorable Martial association into conflict, sacrifice, or a compulsory lesson in cooperation.
The passage does not identify assertiveness as the cause of being liked.

### Jupiter in VI

**Passage:** The judgment is adverse; a waxing Moon without adverse planetary influence mitigates it, while skilled work still faces reversals.
[Firmicus III.3.13–14, printed p. 81 / PDF p. 85](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=85).

**Authoring question:** Can hope remain appealing when competence fails to secure dependable conditions?
This challenges a sixth-house Jupiter entry that automatically turns work into growth or service into moral reward.
A Prince could want relief, better terms, or simple pleasure after work.
No inherited social judgment or illness prediction is needed to make those stakes concrete.

### Jupiter in XII

**Passage:** Benefics in XII do not deliver their benefits.
[Valens II.5, middle paragraph, printed p. 99](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/book02/05-12th.tex).

**Authoring question:** What sustains a wish for help when help does not arrive?
Faith, disappointment, secrecy, dependence, and refusal offer different directions to test.
The evidence challenges automatic hidden protection; it does not establish an inward capacity for resilience.
This is a judgment about the benefic class that includes Jupiter, rather than a separately developed Jupiter portrait.

## Boundaries and source gaps

Firmicus III.13 lacks Moon V–VIII; its XI entry points back to the missing V entry.
[Bram, printed p. 114 / PDF p. 118](https://classicalastrologer.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/firmicusmaternustheoryandpractice.pdf#page=118).
Do not claim this source supplies twelve surviving Moon delineations.

Valens's ninth-place Mercury passage describes an aspect; his third-place Venus passage includes rulership conditions.
[Valens II.7, ninth-place subsection, printed p. 102](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/book02/07a-9th.tex), [II.13, printed p. 108](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/janegca/latex-valens/main/book02/13-3rd.tex).
Neither should be silently substituted for a simple occupancy reading.
Likewise, a statement about an exact angle should not be reassigned to an entire whole-sign house without explaining the change.

The university-hosted Riley PDF failed to load; the inspected Griscti edition supplied the passages instead.
The 2022 Valens PDF URL also failed, so its pagination and wording were not verified.
Lilly's natal material and alternative Firmicus translations were not independently read in this pass and provide no evidence here.

These comparisons are invitations to broaden authorship, not instructions to reproduce a historical hierarchy of worthy and unworthy people.
Do not turn enslavement, gender, sexuality, disability, poverty, or illness into a moral diagnosis.
Preserve the distinction between what a source predicts, what contemporary authors interpret, and what the Prince might want.
