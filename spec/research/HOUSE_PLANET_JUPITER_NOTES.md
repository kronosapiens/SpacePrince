# Jupiter house research notes

Preliminary readings for Houses I–IX and XI–XII, with an independent Mercury IX overlap.
The [full matrix](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md) contains the lead synthesis after review; proposals here preserve the independent research stage.
Consulted 2026-10-04 without reading the current house–planet matrix, continuation, pilot scenes, or new Mercury notes.
The [house reference](../concept/HOUSE_MATRIX.md), [planet framework](../concept/PLANET_MATRIX.md), [Jupiter voice](../concept/PLANETS.md#8-jupiter), and [emotional guidance](../concept/DRIVES.md) supply game context.
Modern readings were established before consulting the historical comparison notes and checking their cited passages.
Adaptations below are original proposals; neither astrological optimism nor adversity guarantees a gameplay outcome.

## I — Self

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes optimistic arrival.
Reed also allows inflated promises under adverse aspects.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want to meet a new situation as someone for whom more is possible.
One expression is infectious confidence without an immediate demand for proof.
Another is enjoying the enlarged expectations his entrance creates.
Either can occur without claiming that confidence ensures success.

## II — Livelihood

**Source notes:** Heese connects resourcefulness, sensory comfort, and excess.
Reed includes lavish spending.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want enough resources to widen what he can enjoy and provide.
One expression is the relief of having provisions beyond immediate necessity.
Another is wanting an impressive abundance because a modest sufficiency feels too small for his plans.
The wish concerns usable possessions, not proof of moral generosity.

## III — Communication

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes sharing ideas, advice, curiosity, and broad connections.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want an ordinary conversation to open more possibilities for people he knows.
One expression is delight in making a useful connection between familiar neighbors.
Another is enjoying the influence of being the person everyone consults.
The latter is our extension of the advisory theme, not Heese's diagnosis.

## IV — Home

**Source notes:** Heese values family and spacious shelter.
Reed also describes oppressive family beliefs under adverse aspects.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want a household large enough to accommodate the life he imagines.
One expression is pleasure in an expanded welcome.
Another is wanting his family to embody an admirable way of living, even when other residents prefer their own customs.
Neither requires a fortunate childhood or inevitable reconciliation.

## V — Creativity

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes warm play and encouragement.
Reed includes excessive provision for children.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want enjoyment to grow beyond its original scale.
One expression is exuberance that gives everyone another reason to participate.
Another is loving the role of patron or encourager so much that he supplies more than was requested.
An extravagant afternoon can simply be delightful.

## VI — Labor

**Source notes:** Heese values respectful work and usefulness.
Reed permits condescension; Firmicus's conditional judgment resists automatic benefit.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want necessary work to offer more than bare endurance, without believing that good work purchases good fortune.
One expression is hope in a concrete improvement to today's conditions.
Another is pride in his generous standards, mixed with irritation when coworkers do not welcome his advice.
These are proposed motives, not deductions from the historical forecast.

## VII — Relationships

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes equality and rewarding partnership.
Reed also warns of exploited trust.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want a partnership because it makes a larger undertaking imaginable.
One expression is gratitude for another person's offer of confidence.
Another is assuming his attractive proposal should be enough to secure their agreement.
The other person's consent and stated terms remain consequential.

## VIII — Transformation

**Source notes:** Heese joins investigation with material sharing.
Reed includes inheritance and disputes around transferred resources.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can imagine possibilities in a shared estate while still wanting someone back who cannot return.
One expression is eagerness to put inherited means to generous use.
Another is expecting the shared resources to support his grander plan because he finds it more worthwhile.
Loss need not become an opportunity he is grateful for.

## IX — Pilgrimage

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes learning and teaching.
Reed also allows dogmatism under adverse aspects.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want an account of the world spacious enough to live by.
One expression is pleasure in studying something that enlarges his possibilities.
Another is enjoying the authority of explaining a belief to others before his own doubts are settled.
Our writing need not turn either stance into a conversion story.

## XI — Friendship

**Source notes:** Heese values groups and helpful friends.
Reed contrasts loyalty with instrumental friendship.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want membership in a circle through which more becomes possible.
One expression is uncomplicated pleasure in accepting help from friends.
Another is wanting access to influential people while also enjoying their company.
Those motives can coexist without revealing that the friendship was false.

## XII — The Hidden

**Source notes:** Heese offers private restoration and hidden protection.
Reed similarly promises help; Valens instead denies benefics their benefits here.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want room for hope when help is neither visible nor assured.
One expression is sustaining a private observance that matters to him.
Another is wanting someone excluded to receive assistance, or wanting the reassurance of being their benefactor.
Neither solitude nor confinement must become spiritually improving.

## Independent overlap — Mercury IX

**Source notes:** Heese emphasizes learning, influential explanation, and advice lacking detail.
Reed adds persistent study and possible dogmatism.

**Original adaptation:** The Prince can want an unfamiliar system of thought to become something he can discuss, translate, and carry elsewhere.
One expression is the pleasure of discovering a distinction his own language misses.
Another is wanting his explanation to move listeners, despite incomplete understanding.
Compared with Jupiter IX's desired orientation in life, this proposal centers the movement and formulation of ideas.

## Gaps

Heese's Jupiter offers little friction; difficult motives are selective original extensions, with Reed supplying some contrasting emphases.
Firmicus and Valens contradict easy optimism without supplying a modern psychology of resilience.
Whether these adaptations engage players remains untested.
