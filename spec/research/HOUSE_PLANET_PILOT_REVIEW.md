# House and planet pilot review

Research and editorial review for [issue #9](https://github.com/kronosapiens/SpacePrince/issues/9), 2026-10-04.
The [pilot](HOUSE_PLANET_PILOT.md) covered eighteen placements; this report preserves its findings and guidance for the remaining 66.
The [full research review](HOUSE_PLANET_REVIEW.md) records the completed continuation; current entries live in the [84-entry reference](../concept/HOUSE_PLANET_MATRIX.md).
The comparison baseline is commit `bbb07bb08162173910c33d52a072af86aaa906e3`.

## Research and synthesis

Separate researchers covered all twelve Saturn placements and all seven Achievement placements, establishing their readings before seeing the existing house–planet scenes or each other's work.
Their [Saturn notes](HOUSE_PLANET_SATURN_NOTES.md) and [Achievement notes](HOUSE_PLANET_ACHIEVEMENT_NOTES.md) preserve those independent proposals and distinguish consulted passages from game adaptations.
The pilot contains the subsequent editorial synthesis.

Cafe Astrology supplied coherent direct-placement coverage; Alice Sparkly Kat's full Saturn series, Suzanne Rough's Achievement readings, Theresa Reed, and Deborah Houlding supplied additional perspectives.
The lead inspected the placement passages and the house discussions used, including the distinctions between natal and transit material, report inserts, and Houlding's own statements versus quoted Lilly.
Nate Craddock's discussion helped distinguish public action from paid occupation and the whole-sign tenth from the Midheaven degree.
Source records contain URLs, authors, consultation date, and passage locators.

The independent Saturn X readings diverged usefully.
One emphasized sustaining a public undertaking partly to keep valued people together; the other emphasized an earned place that no longer needs constant justification.
The synthesis retains public authority as the common stake and lets attachment complicate handing it on.
It does not present the two readings as evidence of a single hidden motive.

## Findings and revisions

An independent reviewer read the complete draft across both axes and checked the original passages in all twelve Alice essays, Cafe's Saturn and Achievement sections, Rough's seven readings and preface, Houlding's X/XII discussions, Craddock, and Reed's Sixth.
The review found one attribution error and two substantive gaps between the proposed distinctions and the scenes.

| Finding | Revision |
| --- | --- |
| Cafe's Venus article explicitly credits Annie Heese, contrary to the initial notes. | Corrected the source overview and Venus entry. |
| Saturn VI promises satisfaction in reliable practice but both scenes depict frustrating work; polishing clean pans also makes refusal seem preselected as sensible. | Replaced the polishing scene with familiar cask work enjoyed through completion; retained the replacement scene's irritation. |
| Sun X and Mars X both refuse honors controlled by somebody else; Mars's “actual decision at hand” remains unspecified. | Mars now chooses the expedition's immediate departure route while its patron keeps the credit; Sun still concerns whose judgment his name represents. |
| Saturn IX describes an empty bowl before an offering has been poured. | The declined offering remains in the bowl. |
| The blind writer reproduced Venus's audience-versus-companion situation almost exactly. | Broadened the second expression and adopted a public rehearsal, where an admired reputation permits the Prince to shape a shared pleasure. |

The same independent reviewer reread the changed material and found both substantive gaps addressed, the attribution and bowl corrected, and the new Venus scene distinct and properly framed as adaptation.
The focused rereview found no new material issue.

The source review upheld the substantive paraphrases and their boundaries, including natal versus transit readings, report inserts, and the difference between Saturn's joy and an actual occupant.
The six sign variations retain their house and planet; Mercury's precise survey versus compared accounts provides the clearest difference in method.
Venus in Aquarius remains a weaker demonstration: unconventional dress conveys less of its relational interpretation than active appreciation of another person's difference would.
That optional refinement is left for user review.
Saturn I and VII also remain comparatively narrow around control and ease; Saturn XII's anticipated judgment needs a concrete opportunity for contact to avoid becoming generic introspection.

## Independent scene exercise

A separate writer received six central dynamics and their two expressions, with no pilot scenes, baseline examples, or research notes.
The selection included likely productive cases, Saturn V and VIII, and harder distinctions, Saturn VII and XII and Venus and Jupiter X.
The writer produced two seeds per interpretation.
The examples below condense those original outputs; the assessment belongs to the lead editor.

| Placement | Fresh scene | What the exercise showed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saturn V | An old traveler offers a chess problem as companions leave for a walk; the Prince declines a hint and spends the remaining light on the problem. | Productive: solitary concentration adds a different pleasure to the pilot's shared dance and public embarrassment. |
| Saturn VII | A beloved interrupts their agreed turns recounting the day to describe a bird stealing her ribbon; he can enforce the turn or ask about the bird while remaining annoyed. | Productive: the exactness of an agreement can obstruct ease without requiring a broken contract or a moral correction. |
| Saturn VIII | An inherited seat requires repeating a dead parent's hostile oath; he can leave the chair empty while still wanting it. | Repetitive: despite different objects, this follows the pilot's conditional bequest closely. |
| Saturn XII | An invitation to a pilgrims' hall reaches him in a stable, but he imagines accusations before anyone has asked a question. | Thin: visible access helps make avoidance concrete, but imagined judgment alone does not sufficiently distinguish the planet. |
| Venus X | An admiring crowd calls him back while a close friend waits behind the platform. | Repetitive: almost the same situation as the pilot, suggesting the supplied second expression overdirected the writer. |
| Jupiter X | As tournament judge, he honors an ambitious failed attempt over the most victorious rider and announces daring as his criterion. | Productive: institutional authority makes his taste for possibility consequential; this remains an original extension of thin direct evidence. |

An additional pass supplied only the central wants, with exclusions to prevent repetition of ceremonial presentation, public praise versus intimacy, and conditional inheritance.
The writer still had not seen the pilot scenes.

| Placement | Additional scene | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venus X | Recognized as a famous puzzle solver at an inn, he enjoys being admitted to a circle of serious puzzlers. | Weak house distinction: reputation mostly grants access to what could otherwise be private play. |
| Venus X | Singers who know his traveling songs appoint him to lead an open rehearsal; he chooses a bawdy round and enjoys directing its pauses. | Adopted in the pilot: recognized leadership changes whose pleasure shapes the public occasion. |

This exercise supports the interpretations' usefulness for writing, with visible failures as well as successes.
It is not a test of how players will actually feel or which mechanical choices they will find engaging.

## Lessons for subsequent batches

- **Give shared drives distinct planetary motives and actions.**
  Recognition can mean authorship for the Sun, acknowledgment from a personally significant witness for the Moon, or freedom to act for Mars.
  State what the Prince wants in this particular situation and what getting it would mean to him.
- **Preserve mixed motives and useful source disagreements.**
  The Prince can want relief while resenting his replacement, or want authority partly to keep collaborators close.
  Reed's enjoyment of service and Alice Sparkly Kat's dedication without enjoyment generated different possibilities for Saturn VI.
  Keep their attribution visible and allow pleasure, difficulty, and unresolved feeling without arranging a corrective lesson.
- **Make the scenes demonstrate the claimed distinctions.**
  Different descriptions of Sun and Mars did not prevent both from initially refusing nominal honors.
  Letting Mars command the departure while another person receives credit made the distinction observable.
  Likewise, an entry promising satisfaction needs a scene in which the task can actually satisfy.
- **Begin blind scene writing with the central dynamic alone.**
  Two expressions can already contain enough of a scene to make independent writers reproduce it.
  Withhold the existing examples and initially the expressions; supply more guidance if needed, recording where it became necessary.
  Compare the resulting wants, actions, and stakes, including repetitive or weak results.
- **Keep the drive vocabulary provisional.**
  State wants such as keeping one's word, preserving dignity, and enjoying competence before assigning categories.
  Let research and scene writing expand or revise the vocabulary when it obscures a useful distinction.

## Assessment of the method

The longer format earns its space when the second expression changes the situation or motive, rather than supplying a dutiful version and an irresponsible version.
Two scenes expose repetition that a single polished example can hide.

For the remaining 66, retain parallel research with an independently studied overlap, one editor comparing across both axes, passage checks, and a blind scene exercise.
Use short entries in the authoring reference and keep disagreements and bibliographic detail in separate notes.
Seek additional traditional direct-placement material before claiming a balanced historical survey; present coverage remains predominantly contemporary, and named practitioners do not establish consensus.
Mercury X's order-versus-variety alternatives and Jupiter X's difficult motives need particular attention in later comparisons.

This pilot does not establish that the player will feel what the Prince feels, or that a broader represented emotional range makes play more compelling.
Those questions require encounter design and playtesting.
The continuation plan and review checkpoints belong in [issue #9](https://github.com/kronosapiens/SpacePrince/issues/9); subsequent batches can test the lessons above before broader guidance is added to [DRIVES](../concept/DRIVES.md).
