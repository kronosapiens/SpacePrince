# Narrative encounter batch review

**Status: Archived experiment, superseded 2026-10-08.**
This combined narrative and economics exercise is retained as a historical record.
Its proposals were not adopted; fresh writing follows the separate narrative review process.
See the [working draft index](../../README.md) for active work.

Review of the [twelve-house writing exercise](ENCOUNTER_BATCH.md), completed 2026-10-08.
The draft tests composition and exposes choices for the narrative refresh; it does not implement or settle new eligibility, prices, or replacement rules.

## Method

The lead wrote V, VI, and X first, using the house, house–planet, and planet–sign references and the agreed economy.
A separate agent reviewed those scenes against the references and current resolver before the lead expanded the batch.
The review combined editorial and economic checks; it was independent of the writer, not two independent reviews by separate specialists.
Original opening fragments were selected from the existing curated YAML, without another external source audit.
No new historical claims are needed to authorize an original scene or price.
After the pilot corrections, the lead wrote the other nine situations and the same reviewer read the complete batch against the references and code.
The lead also checked coverage, paired ordinary terms, chart relationships, quotation matches, and local links with a temporary validation script.
The final checks passed for all 134 written choice rows, all paired ordinary terms, all twelve stored quotation matches, and 93 local links across the two drafts and repository index.
Whitespace checks passed; no runtime code changed and no gameplay or population-balance test was performed.

## Decisions exposed by the pilot

Mercury's Labor offer and Mars's joy are not interchangeable upgrades.
In Virgo, Mercury earns 60 Light for 36 chosen affliction; Mars joy earns 36 for 24 affliction on Mars.
The extra 24 Light equals the baseline price of removing their 12-affliction difference, but target choice, available margin, and current resources still matter.
In Pisces, Mercury earns only 12 more Light for that 12 extra affliction.
A priority rule removes a useful alternative in either version.

The 12-Light dignity increment is small beside growth prices and large beside recovery prices.
Venus in Libra V reduces recovery from an ordinary 48 to 24: half the baseline price, with 12 of the saving supplied by dignity beyond the neutral occupant offer.
Repeated access must be tested before describing this as a modest cumulative advantage.

The proposed occupant rule survives combustion when another planet can receive the effect.
That preserves a natal approach but can make the same planet's changing joy condition irrelevant: Venus's occupant offer already equals or improves upon its joy discount in V.
This needs an explicit design choice, not an assumption hidden in implementation.

## What the batch demonstrates

House structure can shape the scene without adding another modifier.
Angular IV is private but lets the Prince choose his arrangement; angular X makes his role public.
Cadent VI leaves the wage and equipment in another person's hands; cadent IX leaves the teaching system outside his control while allowing real inquiry.
The adverse Second and Eighth remain distinct: earning provisions and settling an inheritance create different stakes even when both involve Light.

Signs can change the social action while ordinary economics stay fixed.
Cancer III makes remembered wording matter; Gemini III offers competing local accounts.
Scorpio IV permits privacy without disclosure; Taurus IV offers tangible familiarity.
The comparison remains linked to occupancy: changing V from Leo to Libra also changes Sun from domicile to fall and Venus from neutral to domicile.
Only the ordinary offers are held economically constant.

The draft allows vanity, appetite, resentment, jealousy, grief, withdrawal, and uncomplicated pleasure.
The Prince can want a friend's attention in XI while the player evaluates a Fortune purchase; neither the gain nor the jealous feeling establishes what the player will experience.
Refusal ends the immediate scene without a correction, hidden debt, or later obligation.
The review establishes readable possibilities, not player emotional responses.

Writing every row did not establish a good combined menu.
VI loses a payable ordinary action when an unaffordable joy replaces it; IX contains a dominated pair; XII suppresses a freely targeted wage in favor of a smaller burden fixed to Saturn.
Testing displayed menus is therefore a separate authoring step, before final prose polish and before coding selection rules.

The draft also repeats settings in which a keeper, teacher, or seller names a price.
II, V, VI, VIII, and XII all pair earning through exertion with paid recovery, despite different motives and situations.
That makes economic comparisons easy but limits the variety of decisions and social relationships.
A later batch should explore more ordinary gifts, alternative payments, and target distributions within the existing outcome vocabulary, without requiring every meaningful human interaction to become a purchase.
This is an editorial direction for a later pass, not a change silently applied after review.

## Coverage and economic limits

There are 24 selected occupant approaches: Sun appears in two houses, Mercury and Jupiter in three each, and Moon, Venus, Mars, and Saturn in four each.
Each approach has copy in both signs, yielding 48 written occupant variants.
The seven joy approaches likewise have two versions each.
This is a writing sample, not coverage of all 84 occupant placements or all 144 house–sign combinations.

Most favorable-dignity increments in the sample are 12 Light beyond the ordinary occupant advantage.
That common increment is a comparison device, not a proposed universal dignity formula.
In V it makes recovery substantially cheaper; in XI it is small beside a Fortune growth price.
For VI, the joy saves 12 affliction against ordinary A, while XII's joy saves 24, with a fixed target in both cases.
Their baseline recovery values are 24 and 48 Light respectively, but restoration price alone does not value target restrictions, available margin, or preserving those planets for later encounters.

Identical ordinary terms within each pair remove sign-driven differences for those two versions, assuming the same encounter frequency.
The draft does not measure a complete catalogue's opportunities across all Ascendant arrangements, accumulated dignity advantages, or birth cohorts.
Mutually exclusive offers are alternatives, not rewards that can be summed.
The changing choice between immediate Light and planetary margin remains important even when nominal Light equivalents match.

Only IV offers revival, one of twelve equally weighted situations.
That is below the [existing content direction](../../../mechanics/MECHANICS.md#10-combustion) of roughly one third to one half of narrative encounters offering revival; actual encounter frequency is not modeled here.
There are no distributed recovery offers in this batch.
Both omissions matter before these drafts could replace the current catalogue.

## Remaining implementation decisions

- **Replacement competition.**
  Decide how to retain meaningful ordinary or special alternatives when several candidates occupy the same position, including VI's unaffordable joy and IX's dominated pair.
  The stated per-scene priorities make the drafts inspectable but do not establish a general selection algorithm.
- **Occupancy and condition.**
  Decide whether an unlocked, combusted occupant can still enable an offer affecting another planet.
  The draft permits it, which can make an overlapping joy condition irrelevant in I, III, V, IX, and XI.
- **Growth and limits.**
  Implement gains in run state, target validity, previews, and atomic outcomes.
  [MECHANICS §10](../../../mechanics/MECHANICS.md#10-combustion) proposes a 180 Resolve maximum, while [Fortune calculations](../../../../client/src/game/combat.ts) clamp at 100%, or 60 sixtieths.
  Decide how an offer promises its full gain near those limits; this exercise neither invents partial pricing nor assumes an uncapped display.
- **Catalogue balance and frequency.**
  Add missing placement and revival coverage, test ordinary opportunity across complete Ascendant arrangements, and assess cumulative occupant and joy advantages over repeated encounters.
  The current scene-selection runtime does not yet select these sign variants or occupant offers.

The next review should use VI and IX to settle how competing offers appear, then revise those combinations before expanding the catalogue or implementing its selection.
The successful scenes can remain useful while those rules change; the research references need not be rewritten to accommodate a menu priority.

## Displayed menu examples

These examples assemble the action and aside tables into the three choices a player would see under the draft's proposed priorities.
Only relevant natal placements are specified; they obey whole-sign relationships without claiming an astronomical birth date.
The stated unlocks, Light, and affliction are example run states, not additional chart attributes.
All seven planets are unlocked in these examples; unspecified planets do not occupy the encounter house and do not change the selected offers.
Previews use the named target for illustration; other eligible targets remain selectable wherever the aside says “chosen.”
The original encounter sections contain the corresponding immediate consequence for every action.

### Creativity with a solar approach and Venus joy

Aries rising places Leo in V, occupied by Sun; Venus is elsewhere in Cancer IV, lit and unafflicted.
The Prince has 36 Light, a lit Sun at 0 affliction with Resolve 108, and Moon in Taurus at 24 affliction with Resolve 72.
V's fixed joy assignment and Venus's current condition enable joy here; its location in IV supplies no Venus V occupant approach.

> A singer has saved the last verse for anyone willing to outdo her ridiculous sea monster, and the listeners have put a purse beside the stage.
> Behind them, an attendant is selling cushions and cool drinks to people content to watch.

| Action | Aside | Example preview |
|---|---|---|
| Give it a voice nobody has heard. | Gain 60 Light; choose a lit planet to take 24 affliction. | Choose Sun: Light 36 → 96; Sun affliction 0 → 24 of 108. |
| Take the place the singer has saved. | Pay 36 Light; remove up to 24 affliction from a chosen lit planet. | Choose Moon: Light 36 → 0; Moon affliction 24 → 0 of 72. |
| Leave before the final verse. | No cost or effect. | Light and planets unchanged. |

The Sun's authorship and Venus's invitation are both legible without naming the chart in the prompt.
The player can prefer either earning or recovery depending on the route ahead; the Prince can simply enjoy performing or sitting beside the singer.

### Labor when the joy cost cannot be paid

Libra rising places Pisces in VI, occupied by Mars alone.
Mars is at 60 affliction with Resolve 72, so joy is present but its 24 cost exceeds the remaining margin of 12.
Moon is in Taurus at 24 affliction with Resolve 72, and the Prince has 36 Light.

> Wet linen arrives faster than the press can empty, and the workers have begun timing their pulls to a song that keeps changing.
> The keeper offers a stack's wage or a paid place beside the warm rinsing basin.

| Action | Aside | Example preview |
|---|---|---|
| Move to the braced handle. | Gain 36 Light; Mars takes 24 affliction. | Disabled: Mars has only 12 remaining margin. |
| Ask for quiet while you recover. | Pay 36 Light; remove up to 24 affliction from a chosen lit planet. | Choose Mars: Light 36 → 0; Mars affliction 60 → 36 of 72. |
| Step out of the rhythm. | No cost or effect. | Light and planets unchanged. |

The suppressed ordinary action, “Join the pulling rhythm,” could have earned 36 Light for 36 affliction on Moon, which has 48 margin.
The proposed replacement therefore removes a usable route to earning without making the encounter impossible to leave.
This is an ordinary reachable state worth designing around, not a reason to silently redirect the joy cost.

### Pilgrimage when one offer supersedes another

Libra rising places Gemini in IX, occupied by Mercury and Jupiter.
The Prince has 216 Light and Moon in Virgo at 12 affliction with Resolve 60.
Mercury is domicile and Jupiter detriment; both special approaches remain eligible.

> At a school above the pass, two teachers disagree about why the same standing practice steadies a traveler.
> They sell a seated lesson or accept a smaller fee from someone willing to demonstrate its long postures while they compare accounts.

| Action | Aside | Example preview |
|---|---|---|
| Try the point where their accounts differ. | Pay 216 Light; give a chosen lit planet +12 Resolve for the rest of the run. | Choose Moon: Light 216 → 0; Resolve 60 → 72; affliction stays 12. |
| Add the practice you learned elsewhere. | Pay 216 Light; a chosen lit planet takes 12 affliction and gains +12 Resolve for the rest of the run. | Choose Moon: Light 216 → 0; Resolve 60 → 72; affliction 12 → 24. |
| Leave them to the comparison. | No cost or effect. | Light and planets unchanged. |

Both purchases give the selected Moon the same gain for the same price; Jupiter's offer additionally afflicts that chosen target and requires enough pre-gain margin.
Its eligible targets are therefore a subset of Mercury's.
Its different fictional motive cannot by itself make this an interesting economic choice.
The draft retains the example as evidence for revising combined menus before adopting the catalogue.
