# House and planet matrix

A working authoring reference for all 84 combinations of seven planets and twelve houses in Space Prince.
Each entry proposes a concern, a concrete scene action, and a tension for a planet occupying that house in the Prince's own chart.
The [house reference](HOUSE_MATRIX.md) supplies situations; the [planet and sign reference](PLANET_MATRIX.md) supplies planetary domains and ways of expressing them.
The examples here are original editorial interpretations for discussion, not implemented encounters or a catalogue of predicted life outcomes.

Houses: [I Self](#i-self) · [II Livelihood](#ii-livelihood) · [III Communication](#iii-communication) · [IV Home](#iv-home) · [V Creativity](#v-creativity) · [VI Labor](#vi-labor) · [VII Relationships](#vii-relationships) · [VIII Transformation](#viii-transformation) · [IX Pilgrimage](#ix-pilgrimage) · [X Achievement](#x-achievement) · [XI Friendship](#xi-friendship) · [XII The Hidden](#xii-the-hidden).

## Reading the entries

The Prince takes the action; the planet identifies the part of the chart through which the Prince participates.
The concern gives the action a reason, while the tension suggests a choice that could develop around it.
A tension need not become a penalty or a moral lesson.
An encounter may end in uncomplicated pleasure, useful work, or welcome relief.

Occupancy is distinct from the house's fixed ruler and its joy.
The fixed ruler continues to supply the encounter's color and musical identity; the occupying planet can supply a particular approach.
A joy is a separate affinity and does not imply that the joy planet occupies this house in the player's chart.
Under whole-sign houses, the Ascendant fixes the sign of each house, and planets sharing a house share its sign.
A sign condition therefore applies to all occupants of that house in a given chart; its additional rarity across charts needs to be measured.

These entries do not assign a compulsory buff to any planet or sign.
The [economy table](../../client/src/data/economy.ts) holds the agreed purchase quantities and prices; particular offers, discounts, gifts, eligibility, and costs remain to be designed.
House occupancy conditions and run-long stat changes are not implemented in the current [narrative resolver](../../client/src/game/narrative.ts).

## Source basis

The [house reference](HOUSE_MATRIX.md#using-this-reference) distinguishes sourced house meanings from the game's editorial situations and preserves differences between its sources.
The [planet frameworks](PLANET_MATRIX.md#planet-frameworks) and individual sign placements provide the other inputs.
All concerns, actions, and tensions in the 84 entries below are our synthesis of those references, not separately sourced natal readings of each combination.
This keeps their proposed game meaning distinguishable from the astrological material used to develop it.

Additional natal-house readings were consulted on 2026-10-04 for Mercury, Venus, and Saturn in the Third and Fifth houses.
These limited checks informed the examples and do not establish a source for every entry in the full matrix.

| Reading | Third-house source theme | Fifth-house source theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Annie Heese, Mercury in the Houses](https://cafeastrology.com/natal/mercuryinhouses.html), corresponding numbered sections | Curiosity, everyday information, learning and sharing | Verbal play, inventive expression, creative teaching |
| [Cafe Astrology, Venus in the Houses](https://cafeastrology.com/natal/venusinhouses.html), corresponding numbered sections | Tact, mediation, pleasing expression | Affection, sensory enjoyment, playfulness |
| [Cafe Astrology, Saturn in the Houses](https://cafeastrology.com/articles/saturninhouses.html), corresponding named sections | Reserve in ordinary communication | Difficulty allowing spontaneity and enjoyment |

The Third- and Fifth-house Saturn entries explore how limits might make participation possible; that constructive encounter treatment is our adaptation of the cited tension.
The Venus article sometimes equates a numbered house with its natural-zodiac sign; this reference keeps house placement and sign placement separate, following the game's existing authoring convention.

Aquarius does not turn all three planets into organizers of the same communal project.
Mercury changes how something is understood, Venus welcomes a distinctive connection, and Saturn makes continued participation workable.
Virgo's attention to detail and Cancer's connection with memory likewise serve different planetary concerns.

The Fifth-house examples retain play and enjoyment even when they involve practice or rules.
The Third-house examples retain an ordinary exchange even when they involve affection or a promise.
These distinctions are useful tests when a combination starts to resemble another house or planet too closely.

## From an entry to an option

Start with the house's situation, choose an occupying planet's concern, then use its sign placement to make the approach specific.
The action provides a possible option; its actual cost, benefit, target, and eligibility still need to be authored.
The occupying planet should have an intelligible part in what is paid, gained, or made possible.

For example, Mercury in Aquarius in Creativity suggests learning a game by changing its rules.
A run-long Mercury stat gain could express that discovery; the choice between Testify and Afflict would depend on what the game teaches and on the encounter's other offers.
Venus in Virgo in Creativity instead suggests relief through an adjustment that makes enjoyment possible.
Saturn in Cancer in Communication suggests relief through accepting help with a familiar obligation.
These are possible reward directions, not priced or approved options.

An ordinary Light purchase starts from the [economy table](../../client/src/data/economy.ts).
A particular placement could justify a discount, a different exchange, or a gift, consistent with the proposed role of rare chart-conditioned benefits.
The source of value needs to be visible in the scene; a planet merely being present need not improve every available offer.
Actual frequency should be checked against chart generation and encounter availability before treating a condition as rare.

An empty house still needs worthwhile baseline decisions.
Several occupants can suggest alternative approaches without requiring additive rewards.
The house's fixed ruler can retain its visual and musical role while these options draw attention to the player's placements.
Whether joys or dignities add separate options or modify these offers remains open.

When developing an encounter from this reference, check that:

- Changing the house changes the immediate situation or stake.
- Changing the planet changes the reason for acting or the kind of action available.
- Changing the sign changes how that planetary concern is expressed.
- The proposed mechanical benefit follows from the action, and the player can see its conditions and price.
- Any burden that matters economically is represented in the actual outcome; prose about time, effort, or risk does not by itself balance a free reward.
- Promises and settlements resolve within the scene unless the game explicitly records a later obligation.

The next useful design step is a small set of complete encounter menus using these entries, with the baseline offer and each placement-conditioned alternative visible together.
That would let us compare the economic value of access, the frequency of the conditions, and the clarity of the writing before deciding how much additional house–sign material is needed.
